Projects, Workforce,
and Education

Civic Tech DC should operate across three complementary program areas: Projects, Workforce, and Education. These areas were inspired by community feedback and reflect the different ways people want to engage with civic technology: contributing to meaningful projects, developing professionally, and learning new skills.

The goal for 2027 is to further develop each program by identifying dedicated leads and establishing programming. Note: rather than launching several new programs at once, the initial focus should be on appointing pillar leads, testing activities within existing Project Nights, and building reusable resources.

01 / ProjectsBack to top ↑

Projects

Projects are the core of Civic Tech DC's programming and will remain central to our work. As such, Project Nights should continue to evolve to reflect community needs, especially in improving how we welcome and engage new contributors.

One of our biggest priorities for 2027 should be improving the newcomer onboarding experience. It can be intimidating for someone attending their first Project Night to immediately join an established project, especially if they are unsure about their skills or not ready to make a long-term commitment.

To address this, we will introduce mini-projects, short, beginner-friendly activities that can be completed in one or two sessions. These could include accessibility audits, documentation improvements, or small prototypes. The goal is to still allow newcomers to contribute something tangible before deciding how they want to participate further. Some ideas include:

  • Looking at how accurately AI tools answer questions about government services, policies, or public information.
  • Explore an open government dataset and create a simple chart, map, or dashboard.
  • Make recommendations for improving the UI of a website.

An Onboarding Lead will oversee the mini-project collection, ensuring that activities are accessible, well-documented, and easy for newcomers to find. Mini-projects will be listed on the Civic Tech DC website, with supporting templates, instructions, and contributions maintained on GitHub.

After completing a mini-project, newcomers can choose to join an existing Civic Tech DC project or continue developing their own ideas through the Civic Tech DC Lab.

The Lab will provide a space for experimentation without requiring a long-term commitment. It will encompass both beginner-friendly mini-projects and working groups, small, time-limited teams that explore specific civic technology questions or challenges.

Working groups are made up of anyone who wants to participate, which can include people already with a project or people who just want to be part of the civic tech field. The groups should focus on topics of current need such as AI in civic technology, government modernization, or community partnerships. Each group would have a designated lead and would have a “design sprint”. Based on their recommendations, working groups would also help create and identify mini-projects that newcomers can take on. For example, an AI working group might develop recommendations for responsible AI use within Civic Tech DC, while newcomers participate in activities evaluating AI tools, documenting use cases, or researching relevant practices.

Every quarter, Civic Tech DC will host project showcases where working groups and mini-project participants can demonstrate their work, discuss challenges, and celebrate contributions.

Leads: All organizers

02 / WorkforceBack to top ↑

Workforce

Many people come to Civic Tech DC to learn new skills, build their resumes, or expand their networks. We want to support these interests because volunteering, while valuable in itself, should also provide meaningful benefits to those contributing their time and expertise.

At the same time, Civic Tech DC is not intended to become a traditional professional networking or recruiting organization. Many groups already serve that purpose. Our value lies in giving members hands-on experience working on real projects and helping them translate that experience into opportunities. Some potential initiatives can include:

Potential initiatives

  • Portfolio and resume workshops: Help volunteers translate project contributions into compelling portfolios and technical case studies.
  • Project-to-portfolio initiative: Encourage project teams to document their work, technical decisions, and impact so volunteers have tangible examples to share with employers.
  • External partnerships: Explore collaborations with Arlington Economic Development, Unstuck Labs, Refraction, and other organizations supporting entrepreneurship, workforce development, and technology careers.

03 / EducationBack to top ↑

Education

The education program area is in response to interest from the community in being able to learn new skills or build upon skills. To accomplish this, we can create a lightweight Civic Tech DC Academy that draws on the expertise already present in our community. This means interested members can serve as volunteer instructors or advisors, offering occasional workshops, demonstrations, and hands-on learning opportunities.

The primary challenge with this is capacity, as creating additional events requires venue space, coordination, and outreach. The Academy should initially operate within existing Project Nights where we can create a separate Luma for the workshop and have that instructor give their workshop during Project Nights. The workshop's recording and materials can then be kept in an Academy Resource Library so members can revisit asynchronously.

Lead: To be identified

Across all three pillarsBack to top ↑

Advisory Board

Alongside the three pillars, we should establish an Advisory Board made up of individuals who can share their expertise. Many people have said they love our work—let’s give them a platform to join. It will be very low commitment for them but offer them an opportunity to contribute to a cause they care about. They can also be our “champions” for funding.
